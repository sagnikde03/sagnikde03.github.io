Automatic speech recognition (ASR) models have become increasingly sophisticated, yet the underlying mechanisms driving their translation accuracy remain underexplored. This article explores the comparative influence of speaker characteristics and content similarity on ASR model performance, utilizing unsupervised distance metrics and clustering algorithms to gain deeper insights.

By conducting a series of experiments using custom datasets, this work examines whether latent space features correlate more with speaker traits, such as accent, pitch, and speaking style, or with the semantic and syntactic content of the speech. The findings reveal significant insights into the biases and strengths of current ASR technologies, highlighting the balance between speaker-dependent and content-dependent factors. Understanding these dynamics enhances the development of more robust and inclusive ASR systems and paves the way for innovations in speech technology applications that better serve diverse populations and varied linguistic contexts.
